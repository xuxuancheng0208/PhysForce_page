Abstract

Interactive video world models aim to predict how visual environments evolve in response to user interventions, including physically consistent state changes induced by applied forces. Existing methods often represent forces as motion trajectories, which may introduce inappropriate motion priors, causing the model to over-rely on encoded displacement patterns. More fundamentally, the model's internal representations do not reliably localize force-induced state changes, making it difficult to associate applied forces with their resulting physical responses. Also, limited force-annotated data make it difficult to produce physically consistent and temporally stable predictions in unseen scenarios.

To address these limitations, we propose PhysForce, a two-stage framework that integrates supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning through three complementary components. During supervised fine-tuning, (1) the Latent Force Intervention Encoder maps force location, direction, and magnitude into the video latent space without exposing future motion; and (2) the Physics Attribution Network associates force interventions with localized changes in the latent state, strengthening the learning of physical responses to applied forces. During reinforcement learning, (3) Physics-Aware Reinforcement Learning uses self-supervised rewards on out-of-distribution videos to improve physical consistency and generalization. Extensive experiments show that PhysForce improves force responsiveness, physical consistency, and generalization, demonstrating its effectiveness for interactive video world models.

Key Contributions

Latent Force Intervention Encoder

LFIE encodes force location, direction, and magnitude in the video latent space without trajectory leakage, allowing the model to predict state changes caused by the applied force.

Physics Attribution Network

PAN learns a force-conditioned response map that localizes state changes associated with the intervention.

Physics-Aware Reinforcement Learning

PARL improves physical consistency and generalization using attribution-guided self-supervised rewards from videos, with a Dynamic Gate mitigating reward hacking.

New State of the Art

PhysForce establishes a new state of the art on both OOD and ID benchmarks, decisively outperforming prior methods in force controllability and physics alignment while maintaining high visual quality and temporal coherence.

Method Overview

Figure 3. Overview of PhysForce. Stage 1 performs force-conditioned supervised fine-tuning with LFIE and PAN, while Stage 2 applies PARL with a self-supervised reward and Dynamic Gate.

Stage 1 · LFIE

The Latent Force Intervention Encoder converts force location, direction, and magnitude into a latent force embedding. Conditioning the video model in latent space avoids exposing future displacement through trajectory prompts.

Stage 1 · PAN

The Physics Attribution Network predicts where the force changes the latent state. An attribution loss aligns these predictions with inter-frame changes in the ground-truth video, explicitly coupling intervention and response.

Stage 2 · PARL

Physics-Aware Region Reinforcement Learning filters unreliable rollouts with a dynamic gate and uses self-supervised, attribution-guided rewards to improve physical consistency and generalization on unseen scenes.
